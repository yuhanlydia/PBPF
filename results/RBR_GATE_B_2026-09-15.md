# RunBugRun real-data Gate B diagnostic — 2026-09-15

## Scope

This diagnostic replaces the flat-likelihood/random-prefix behavior of the
legacy real-model scripts for the **prediction gate only**. It trains the
repository's `NeuralBeliefModel` with FIVO plus future-outcome NLL and evaluates
candidate-specific particle posteriors after four visible executions. It does
not train the actor soft-prefix projector and is not a repair pass@1 result.

The data are the checksum-verified official RunBugRun v0.0.1 Python and test
files. Problems are disjoint between fitting, checkpoint selection, and test.
Every buggy and fixed program is executed on ten deterministically ordered
tests; records whose fixed program does not pass all ten are removed.

| Split | Candidates | Source problems |
|---|---:|---:|
| Train | 282 | 112 |
| Development | 54 | 27 |
| Test | 79 | 66 |

There are 474 held-out future outcomes on test (six per candidate). The model
uses 8 particles, a 32-dimensional latent, and frozen 256-dimensional signed
lexical-content features. Candidate/task identifiers are not model inputs.

## Three-seed result

Values are mean ± sample standard deviation over seeds 1701–1703.

| Arm | NLL ↓ | Brier ↓ | Accuracy ↑ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| No visible evidence | 1.094 ± 0.024 | 0.638 ± 0.008 | 49.30% ± 2.32% |
| Visible history rate | 0.819 ± 0.000 | 0.393 ± 0.000 | **82.07% ± 0.00%** |
| **PBPF** | **0.542 ± 0.043** | **0.302 ± 0.017** | 78.76% ± 1.16% |
| Shuffled test/outcome pairing | 0.518 ± 0.013 | 0.293 ± 0.010 | 80.10% ± 1.58% |
| Wrong-candidate evidence | 0.739 ± 0.052 | 0.400 ± 0.023 | 72.57% ± 0.92% |
| Random matched-radius latent | 1.132 ± 0.014 | 0.669 ± 0.008 | 41.14% ± 3.49% |

PBPF improves NLL over no visible evidence by 0.552 on average and over the
history-rate baseline by 0.277. For every seed, the problem-cluster bootstrap
95% interval for PBPF's NLL gain over both comparators is above zero. The
history-rate arm has slightly higher raw accuracy but is substantially worse on
both proper scoring rules, reflecting overconfident errors.

Wrong-candidate evidence consistently loses 0.197 NLL relative to PBPF. On the
23 candidates belonging to a test problem with multiple candidate versions,
wrong-candidate NLL is 1.15–1.28 while PBPF is 0.49–0.58. Random latent also
removes the gain. This is evidence that the learned state contains useful
candidate-specific outcome information.

## Gate decision

**The full preregistered causal Gate B does not pass.** Shuffling the association
between the first four test descriptors and outcomes does not remove the gain;
it is marginally better than PBPF in all three seeds. About 64.6% of test
candidates have the same outcome on all four visible cases, so this dataset
mostly rewards an exchangeable summary of the candidate's failure distribution.
On the 28 nonconstant candidates, PBPF beats the history-rate NLL in two seeds
and is approximately tied in one, but shuffling remains as good or better.

The defensible result is therefore narrower than the complete PBPF claim:

> Four observed executions can be compressed into a learned candidate-specific
> latent that predicts future execution outcomes substantially better than no
> evidence, a smoothed outcome histogram, wrong-candidate evidence, or a random
> latent on unseen RunBugRun problem families.

The experiment does not yet show that particle structure tracks which test
produced which outcome, and it does not establish that conditioning a frozen
actor on the state improves repair success.

## Reproduction

The executable is `scripts/run_rbr_prediction_gate.py`. The immutable prepared
cache and Torch checkpoints stay local; the compact aggregate and per-seed JSON
reports are versioned. The exact official source-file MD5 values are embedded in
the runner and validated before preparation.
